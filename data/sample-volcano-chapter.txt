Volcanoes: Mountains That Can Erupt

A volcano is an opening in Earth’s crust where melted rock, gases, and small pieces of rock can reach the surface. Melted rock below the ground is called magma. When it reaches the surface, it is called lava. Many volcanoes look like mountains because layers of cooled lava, ash, and rock build up around the opening, called a vent.

Deep inside Earth, rock can melt because of heat and changes in pressure. Magma is lighter than the solid rock around it, so it can rise through cracks. Gases are mixed into the magma. As magma rises, pressure on it decreases and the gases expand, a little like bubbles growing when a fizzy drink is opened. The rising magma and expanding gas can push hard enough to break through the crust and cause an eruption.

Eruptions can look very different. Runny lava may flow slowly down a volcano. In a more explosive eruption, gas can blast ash, hot rock, and volcanic gases high into the air. Ash can travel far in the wind. Fast, hot clouds of gas and rock can rush down the volcano’s sides. Scientists watch earthquakes, ground movement, gas, and heat to understand when a volcano may be changing.

Volcanoes can be dangerous, but they also create new land and rich soils. Learning how they work helps communities prepare and stay safe.
